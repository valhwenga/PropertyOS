# Blueprint reconciliation and MVP completion checklist

Reconciles *PropertyOS Product and Technology Blueprint v0.1* (3 October 2026)
against the implementation, and tracks what remains. Maintained as work lands —
a row moves only when the evidence named in it exists.

Blueprint §34 is the standard this document is held to: *"A feature that
displays mock totals is not complete finance functionality"*, and *"Do not
permit a coding assistant to declare the whole product production ready merely
because the pages render."*

## Completion scale

Every row carries one of four states. They are not degrees of confidence; they
are different kinds of evidence.

| State | Means |
|---|---|
| **Tested** | Implemented, with automated tests over the business rule, run and passing. |
| **Exercised** | Also driven through the running application by a person or a browser test, not only through its domain function. |
| **External** | Also exercised against the real third-party service it will use in production. |
| **Open** | Not built, or built without the evidence above. The note says which. |

A module at **Tested** is not finished. Three of the defects found in this
project — dead links to pages that were never built, an email path that silently
reached nobody, a PDF that rendered a fallback — all passed their domain tests.

## 1. Conflicts between the blueprint and the implementation

Points where the two genuinely disagree, and what was done.

| # | Blueprint | Implementation | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| C32 | §5 *Effective-dated charge schedules*, §14 on the agreement being the source of truth, §34 on screens that mislead. | `charge_schedules` has been effective-dated since the first migration and its own comment says what for — *"a rent increase closes the old row and opens a new one; the historical amount stays readable"* — and **nothing ever wrote a second row**. A rent schedule was written once at activation and could not be changed; a recurring parking bay, levy or fixed utility could only be created by the CSV import. The consequence was not cosmetic: the lease agreement this product generates promises the rent increases by the escalation in its own terms on each anniversary, `leases.escalation_percent` and `escalation_month_interval` record it, and the billing run went on raising the original amount for the whole term — **the product billing a figure that contradicted the agreement it printed**. Two further defects surfaced while testing it: the billing run collapsed every non-rent schedule to the category `other`, so a water recovery and a parking bay reached a tenant's invoice under the same meaningless word and credited the same income account; and an `adjustment_debit` could not be sent, so a landlord raising a levy had to tell the tenant some other way. | **Resolved.** Migration 0062 adds `supersedes_schedule_id` and `change_reason`, and `packages/domain/src/charge-schedules.ts` adds, changes and stops a recurring charge: a change closes the old row the day before and opens a new one pointing back at it, so the months either side of an increase read as the two amounts they actually were. It refuses a change backdated onto its own start (which would restate what was already billed), a second charge of one category over the same dates (the exclusion constraint, reported in words), and switching rent off — ending a lease is a different act. `escalationsDue` reads the agreement's own percentage, interval and start date, works out which anniversary has passed, compares it with what the schedule is still billing, and **proposes** the arithmetic; nothing applies it. Software that raises somebody's rent on its own is software nobody should run. The lease screen shows the schedule history with three states (current, waiting to start, historical — a future row calling itself "historical" was a defect the browser test caught), and the escalation notice carries the percentage, the date it fell due, what is still being billed and an editable figure. Billing now posts the schedule's real category and picks the document type from it, and an `adjustment_debit` is sendable. |
| C31 | §5 on charges, §8 on what a resident is shown, §34 on screens that mislead. | An invoice could only ever say "rent". There was **no screen for any ad-hoc charge at all**, so a landlord could bill a fixed monthly amount through a schedule and nothing else — which covers rent and covers none of water, electricity, sewerage or refuse, each of which moves every month because it is a meter reading times a municipal tariff. A tenant querying R385.80 got a line called `utility_fixed` and no way to check it. Separately, a credit note existed in the ledger and nowhere a tenant could see it: somebody charged in error and credited back saw only the charge. And a deposit — the one piece of money in the product a tenant could not see at all — was a single figure labelled "held", with no account of interest, deductions or refunds, which is precisely the document somebody needs at the end of a lease. | **Resolved.** Migration 0061 gives a charge line the arithmetic behind its amount (quantity, unit, tariff, and the meter pair), with database constraints that refuse half a calculation or a single reading, and widens the category list to name water, electricity, sewerage (domestic effluent), refuse, gas, levies and the rest. **Nothing recomputes a posted line**: the operator enters what the municipality charged, the reading is stored as evidence, and the invoice prints `meter 4312 to 4324 · 12 kl × R32.15` — and says so where the two do not agree rather than preferring either. A lease now has a *Raise a charge* panel (behind `billing.post`) that opens the meter fields only for the categories that have meters. Credit notes render in all three designs: "CREDIT NOTE", the amount as a positive with the direction in words, no due date, the invoice it corrects and the reason, no banking details and the reference kept — and an invoice that is partly credited says how much, because the figure above it is no longer what is owed. `app.resident_deposit_history` and a deposit statement PDF account to a tenant for their own deposit — every receipt, every interest credit, every deduction with the reason recorded for it — and deliberately return no approver and no internal note. Finally, the documents stopped calling themselves **tax invoices**: the words have a meaning in the VAT Act, no tax is calculated here, and most residential letting is an exempt supply. `docs/scopes/vat.md` is what would have to be true to use them. ADR 0016. |
| C30 | §8 on what a resident is shown. §13 on what is sent to them. §34 on screens that mislead. | There was no invoice. A billing run posted charge documents, and the only document the product could produce for a tenant was a **statement** — which answers a different question: a statement says what an account has done and moves every time money does, where an invoice says what was charged on one date and what reference to quote. A tenant asking "please send me the invoice" could be sent neither. | **Resolved.** `renderInvoicePdf` and three designs — Classic, Modern, Compact — chosen in Settings → Appearance with a specimen rendered in the organisation's own colours (invented figures, an invented tenant, SAMPLE on its face). Every design carries the landlord's registered name, registration and VAT numbers and address; the property by name, unit and street; each line with proration shown as the arithmetic; the amount and due date; and the lease reference labelled as the thing to quote — because that is what `suggestAllocation` matches a bank line on. `tests/unit/invoice-layout.test.ts` reads the text back out of all three PDFs and fails if a design loses one of them. Migration 0060 adds the design key and `app.resident_invoice_landlord`, so a tenant reading their own invoice gets the same facts from a table they cannot read — the same ones already printed on the lease they signed. No VAT is calculated (`charge_lines` has no tax columns and a rate nobody entered is not a number to file a return on), no balance is shown, and the full account number stays behind the audited reveal rather than being printed on the most forwardable document the product makes. The email gains `{{invoice.link}}` rather than an attachment. ADR 0016. |
| C29 | §34 on screens that mislead, read against what a white-label product promises. | A landlord could set their accent, typeface and logo, and the product still said Spike everywhere it mattered most: the **statement PDF header** drew the Spike wordmark in purple on the document a resident keeps and forwards to a bank; the browser tab and the icon in a bookmark read `Spike PropertyOS`; and the sign-in page their staff open several times a week was ours. The PDF writer had no image support at all, so a logo could not have been drawn even where the design called for one. | **Resolved.** `packages/integrations/src/image.ts` reads a logo well enough to embed it — baseline JPEG passed through as `DCTDecode`, PNG inflated, unfiltered through all five scanline predictors, palettes expanded, alpha carried as an `/SMask` — and refuses, as null, everything it cannot embed faithfully: 16-bit, interlaced, progressive JPEG, CMYK, SVG, a corrupt `IDAT`. A refusal is not an error: the statement header falls back to the landlord's name in their accent colour, because a statement must never fail to generate over a decoration. Attribution becomes one 7pt line at the foot of the page. Tab titles and favicons are now per organisation in the console and per landlord in the portal; the portal shell shows the landlord's mark where the resident has exactly one tenancy and stays neutral where they have two. Migration 0059 adds `app.sign_in_branding(slug)` and `app.sign_in_logo(slug)` for `/sign-in/<slug>`, a page that renders with no session at all: both return a colour, a typeface and bytes, and **no name** — an unknown slug and a suspended customer are both indistinguishable from an organisation that chose nothing, so the URL cannot be walked to find out who this product's customers are. ADR 0015. `Powered by Spike Technologies` stays in the portal footer, by choice. |
| C28 | §8 *The resident portal shows the statement, the lease and shared documents.* §9 *Show verified banking details in the portal.* §12 on maintenance. §34 on screens that mislead. | A design pass against the brief found six things the portal was getting wrong, three of them about money. The balance was a single figure labelled "Due now" that included a rent invoice raised that morning — money nobody had yet failed to pay. The evidence card said "We have your payment", which is what a bank statement says, not what an uploaded screenshot establishes. A resident could see the last four digits of the account to pay but not the number, so a first payment was impossible without the paperwork. The maintenance list had no detail page, so "when is somebody coming?" had no answer; the emergency line printed the landlord's office number. Allocation rows showed one R7,500 receipt as two entries. The statement had no totals and no period. | **Resolved.** The balance is three measures — outstanding, overdue, not yet due — with the oldest and next due dates and an explicit "as at". Evidence reads "We received your proof of payment for R…, bank confirmation is pending", with a progress list built only from `evidence_status`, `reviewed_at`, `review_note` and whether a receipt exists; a rejected claim shows the recorded reason and offers resubmission. Migration 0048 adds three narrow `security definer` reads and an `emergency_contact` column: the full account number is returned to the DOMAIN layer only, opened under `revealResidentAccountNumber` on explicit request, with an audit entry naming the account and never the number — it is not in the page as served. The statement gained opening/charges/payments/closing totals that are asserted to reconcile, a period that carries into the PDF, and receipt grouping that refuses to group across dates or to swallow a reversal. Maintenance gained a detail page reading the real `maintenance_ticket_events` history, resident-visible comments and attachments, and the work order's appointment and contractor through a function that returns neither the spending ceiling nor the approver. Navigation became a bottom bar on a phone, respecting the safe-area inset, with the shell padded so the footer clears it. |
| C27 | §8 on the resident portal. §34 on screens that mislead. | The portal was one page carrying everything, which is right on a phone and wrong on a laptop: a statement was four sections of scrolling away and had no address anybody could be sent to, and every visit paid for building the statement whatever the resident came for. Separately, the proof-of-payment form computed `new Date()` **inside a client component**: the server rendered its UTC date and the browser re-rendered its local one, so React reported a hydration mismatch it says it will not patch up. The visible half was worse than the warning — between midnight and 02:00 in Johannesburg the form offered yesterday, and the field refuses a future date, so it then refused the real one. | **Resolved.** Five sections — Overview, Statement, Requests, Documents, Your lease — wrapping into two lines on a phone and stacking into a sidebar from `md` up, with the tenancy resolved once in the layout above them. Each page now runs only its own queries. The date is resolved on the server in the organisation's own time zone and handed to the form as a prop; `todayIn` moved into its own module so a page, a route handler and the report filters cannot disagree about what day it is. |
| C26 | §8 *The resident portal shows the statement, the lease and shared documents.* §9 *Show verified banking details in the portal.* §12 on maintenance. | The portal answered "what do I owe" and "what does my lease say", and nothing else. It asked the resident to pay and **never said where**: §9's verified banking details were not shown anywhere, so a resident who received an email claiming the banking details had changed had nowhere authoritative to check. A resident could report a problem and then never see it again — no screen showed what had happened to it. Every document it listed linked to `/portal/<lease>/documents/<id>`, **a route that had never been built**, so all of them answered 404; the link checker signs in as an operator, and an operator never opens this page. The portal home selected every lease RLS allowed rather than the viewer's own portal links, so a landlord who also rents from their own agency would have opened the portal and found the portfolio in it. Neither widens a policy, because both rows carry something that must not travel — the sealed account number, and the landlord's sealed identity number. The account number is **not** returned at all: a resident confirms the last four digits of details they already hold, which is what the anti-redirection check needs, and unsealing the full number for a web page is a decision with its own blast radius rather than a side effect of a design pass. Only a **verified** account is shown; an unverified one produces an honest absence. The portal now also shows the resident's own requests with their status and the latest resident-visible update, the document route exists, the home page is scoped to the viewer's own links, and below `sm` the statement is a list rather than a table. |
| C25 | §4 *Separation of duties*, §34 *a figure is never read bare.* | The overview's **pending approvals** tile counted `deposit_events` where `approved_at is null` — a row the database forbids, because `deposit_events_refund_controls` requires an approver on every deduction and refund. The term was always zero. C13 fixed the approvals *queue* when payout requests got their own table and left the *count* behind, which is the worse half: a clerk raises a payout, the queue shows it, and the dashboard an approver actually looks at says there is nothing to do. Separately the **arrears** tile had always linked to the ageing report with `overdue=true`, which the report ignored — the number counted money past its due date and the page it opened listed every unpaid charge, this morning's invoice included. | **Resolved.** The count reads `deposit_payout_requests`, with a test that fails on the old query. `arrearsAgeing` honours `overdueOnly`, the flag appears as a control on the report, and the qualification says what was left out. Three queues that existed in the data and on no screen were added: a supplier cost approved and unpaid, a file nothing has scanned, and a lease signed off that nobody activated — which bills nobody. The expenses screen is paged and filtered by status, and a status asked for from the dashboard shows the whole queue whatever its date: a payables queue narrowed to this month is not a payables queue. |
| C24 | §26 *Target ordinary page data reads under one second against 50 organisations, 5,000 units and five years of charges.* §34 on screens that mislead. | Leases, residents and maintenance read **every row in the organisation** and filtered in JavaScript, on screens a letting agent keeps open all day. The documents list cut itself off at the most recent 200 and said nothing, which on screen is indistinguishable from having 200 documents; the expenses list did the same at 500. None of the four had a search box, a filter or a page. An archived resident could not be reached from anywhere in the product: the query excluded them unconditionally and nothing could ask. | **Resolved.** Filtering, searching and paging moved into SQL for all four, with `count(*) over ()` returning the size of the filtered set in the same round trip, so the line under each list says what it is showing out of what exists. Archived residents are behind a filter rather than behind nothing. The quarantine banner on Documents counts **every** held file, not the ones on this page: a safety statement that silently narrows to the current page is worse than none. Search terms are escaped, so a search for `100%` is a search for `100%` and not for everything. The expenses screen keeps its cap for now and states it. |
| C23 | §14 *A management snapshot should be reproducible from the underlying records.* §34 *A figure is never read bare.* | Every report read `from` and `to` from the URL and offered **no control that could set them**, so the period was whichever calendar month the server was in and the page never said which. The CSV route then parsed the query string a second time on its own and called five of the eight reports with **no filters at all**: a file downloaded from a narrowed screen could quietly contain everything, filed under a name claiming a period it never had. `ReportFilters.propertyIds` was honoured by four reports and offered by none. A malformed date reached PostgreSQL as `'banana'::date` and answered 500. | **Resolved.** One resolver and one runner (`apps/web/src/lib/report-shapes.ts`, `reports.ts`) used by both the screen and the export, so they cannot answer differently. Each report declares its shape — a range, an as-at date, a forward horizon, or a position that only exists now — and **only the controls it honours are rendered**: the arrears ageing is computed against the records as they stand, so it is offered no date to accept and discard, and journal lines are never narrowed by property because a bank receipt belongs to no single property and the extract would stop balancing. The report says so on its face. Property narrowing added to the deposit register, lease expiry and occupancy; the horizon and the property names now travel into the exported file in words, beside the exact JSON. An unreadable date falls back to the default **and says it did**. Also fixed: the default month was the server's UTC month, not the customer's. |

| C21 | §24 *A customer organisation is provisioned with its own administrator.* | `createOrganisationWithOwner` has existed since the first migration with no screen, so every organisation in existence was created by the seed or by a test. | **Resolved.** `provisionCustomer` in `packages/domain/src/platform.ts` and a screen at `/platform/new`. A unique index, not the screen, is what stops one person signing twice. | M12 | §15 *Billing needs draft preview, validation report, approval, post and downloadable batch summary.* | **Exercised.** The batch summary is a CSV carrying who prepared the run, who posted it, the preview version, and the caveat that posted charges are immutable. Only a posted run has one: a preview is not a batch. |
| M11 | §9 *A suggested match is not automatically final.* | **Exercised.** Suggesting and applying are separate actions; the suggestion fills the form and the operator confirms or changes it. The stored policy records `manual` whenever a person confirmed the amounts, because the applied policy affects arrears ageing. |
| M10 | §19 *Require MFA for Spike administrators and finance approvers.* | **Exercised**, and a person can now enrol one themselves: the only path used to be `pnpm db:mfa enrol` against the database, so an invited finance approver could sign in, correctly see almost nothing, and have no way out. Also stronger: §4's *"exceptional actions require fresh authentication and an audit reason"* is enforced in the database for banking changes. Re-verification is implemented for the local auth provider only; on Supabase it refuses honestly rather than pretending. |

## 3. Where the implementation is ahead of the blueprint

Recorded so it is not mistaken for scope creep.

- **Lease agreement generation.** A 19-page master lease, 75 merge fields, PDF
  generation and in-browser preview. The blueprint puts templates in Phase 2
  (§5). Built at the user's explicit request from their own source documents.
- **MFA-aware permissions.** `app.has_permission()` requires `aal2` for gated
  roles, enforced in the database. §19 asks for MFA for administrators and
  finance approvers; this is stricter and enforced lower down.
- **Support access transparency.** Time-limited, audited, with a visible
  history screen. §19 asks for it; §4 does not require the operator-facing view.

## 6. What cannot be verified here, and why

Stated plainly, because the blueprint asks for exactly this and the user's
instructions repeat it.

- **No bank feed and no statement import.** Every receipt in the product was
  entered by a person who saw the money in the account. Nothing invents a
  receipt, and nothing turns a resident's claim into one.
- **No external service has ever been contacted.** `docs/credentials.md` is the
  register of what that leaves unverified, variable by variable, and
  `pnpm preflight` reports the same thing about whichever environment it is run
  in. Email, payment, signature
  and accounting adapters have never run against a real provider. §24's
  qualification column is unanswered for every integration. In particular
  **PropertyOS never contacts a bank**: a bank account marked verified was
  marked so by a person, and the screen says which of the four methods they
  used rather than the bare word "verified".
- **Re-verification is implemented for the local development auth provider
  only.** Under `AUTH_PROVIDER=supabase` it refuses with an explanation rather
  than silently granting freshness. Sensitive changes are therefore unavailable
  on a Supabase deployment until that path is built and tested against the real
  service.
- **No production deployment, and no staging environment exists.** Everything
  below is from a local development container.
- **A restore has been rehearsed, at development scale only.** It passes 18
  automated checks and the application comes up against it, but the §22 recovery
  *targets* remain aspirations: a 1-second restore of a 672 KB database on a
  local machine is not a recovery time. No object backup exists, so document
  bytes have never been recovered from one.
- **No legal review** of the lease, notices, deposit rules or money flow. This
  matters most for deposits: PropertyOS records who approved a deduction and on
  what evidence, and deliberately encodes no rule about which deductions,
  interest treatment or refund deadlines are lawful. §21 requires counsel to
  settle those before the feature is relied on.
- **No accountant has validated the chart of accounts or posting model.** §10
  requires it before the financial model is used commercially. Migration 0040
  added an accounts payable account on that same unreviewed basis.
- **Load measured once, on a container.** §26's targets now have figures
  against the blueprint's dataset (`docs/performance.md`), and four screens
  miss them. What remains unknown is everything about concurrency, write paths
  and production-class hardware.

Until these are closed, this product is at the stage the blueprint calls a
controlled pilot candidate — and not yet that, because the rent workflow in
§5 cannot be completed through the interface.
