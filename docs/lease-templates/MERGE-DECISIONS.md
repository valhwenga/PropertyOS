# Where the two sources disagreed, and what the master does

Eleven real conflicts. Each is recorded with what each document said, what the
master does, and why — so you can overturn any of them rather than discover them
later in a dispute.

**None of this is legal advice.** The master is a careful merge of two documents
you supplied, checked for internal consistency. Whether it holds up is for an
attorney to say, and the document says so on its face.

The short version: where the two disagree, the master follows **source 2** almost
everywhere, because source 2 was drafted against the Acts by name and source 1
predates or ignores them. Source 1 contributes substance that source 2 simply does
not cover.

---

## 1. Can the landlord cancel because the property is sold?

- **Source 1, clause 2:** yes — one month's notice to the tenant to vacate.
- **Source 2, clause 40.1:** no — "a sale does not by itself cancel this lease".
- **Master: follows source 2.**

*huur gaat voor koop* — a lease survives a sale, and the buyer takes the property
with the tenant in it. A clause letting the landlord end a fixed term on a month's
notice because a buyer appeared is the kind of term a tribunal looks at hard. The
master keeps source 2's obligation to disclose the tenancy to the purchaser and
hand over the deposit with a written account of principal and interest.

**To overturn:** say so, and I will put source 1's clause back as clause 40.4.

## 2. Self-help on arrears — cutting services, forcing entry, removing goods

- **Source 1, clause 17.1:** the landlord may disconnect electricity and water,
  force entry, and remove the tenant's contents to storage, on the tenant's signed
  acknowledgement that "a court order is not necessary".
- **Source 2, clauses 11.4, 22.3, 33.1:** forbidden. Services must not be cut to
  force payment or departure; lockouts, seizure and cutoffs are "not permitted
  substitutes for legal process"; any security right must be enforced through the
  lawful process.
- **Master: follows source 2. Source 1's clause is not in the master.**

This is the one I would not transcribe without telling you. Spoliation, the Rental
Housing Act and PIE all point the same way, and a signature does not generally cure
it. Source 2 was evidently written knowing this.

**DECIDED: removed**, on the owner's instruction of 8 October 2026. The drift test
now fails any published template version that reintroduces service disconnection
or says a court order is unnecessary, so it cannot come back by accident.

## 3. Is the deposit held in an interest-bearing account?

- **Source 1, clause 25:** "held in a non-interest bearing account".
- **Source 2, clause 13.1:** interest-bearing, interest credited at not less than
  the applicable savings rate, proof on request.
- **Master: follows source 2.**

Section 5 of the Rental Housing Act requires an interest-bearing account and
interest for the tenant. Source 1's wording does not comply.

## 4. How long until the deposit comes back?

- **Source 1, clause 25:** 45 calendar days after vacating, flat.
- **Source 2, clause 13.4–13.5:** 7 days if nothing is owed; 14 days after
  restoration where there are lawful deductions; 21 days where the tenant did not
  attend the outgoing inspection.
- **Master: follows source 2**, and keeps source 1's *reason* for a longer wait —
  utilities billed in arrears — as a recorded ground for a lawful deduction and
  reconciliation rather than a blanket 45-day hold.

## 5. Interest on arrears

- **Source 1, clause 14.1:** 1.5% per month, capitalised monthly (≈19.6% a year
  compounding), or the maximum legal rate, whichever is less.
- **Source 2, clause 10.4:** simple interest, only if a lawful rate is completed in
  the schedule, subject to all limits and required notices; "no interest rate is
  created by an uncompleted field".
- **Master: follows source 2**, with the rate as a schedule field.

Compounding monthly runs into the in duplum rule and the National Credit Act. The
master also keeps source 2's annual cap field.

## 6. May the tenant cancel early?

- **Source 1:** silent — the tenant is bound for the full term.
- **Source 2, clause 21:** 20 business days' written notice, with a reasonable and
  calculated cancellation charge, mitigation, and no double recovery.
- **Master: follows source 2.**

Section 14 of the CPA gives a consumer this right; a lease that omits it does not
remove it.

## 7. Occupancy limits

- **Source 1, clause 12:** no more than 2 adults and 2 minor children overnight.
- **Source 2, item 1.5:** a completed maximum, with permitted occupants named.
- **Master: follows source 2**, as a schedule field, because a template that hard-codes
  2+2 is wrong for every family that is not 2+2.

## 8. Three complaints and the lease ends

- **Source 1, clause 12:** more than 3 written complaints from anyone in the
  complex lets the landlord terminate with immediate effect.
- **Source 2, clauses 9.2 and 23:** a fine or rule breach must be lawful, evidenced,
  attributable and open to challenge; cancellation needs notice and a remedy period.
- **Master: follows source 2.** A complaint count from unnamed neighbours is not a
  finding of fact, and "immediate effect" bypasses the remedy period.

## 9. Renewal

- **Source 1, clause 2:** option exercised by the tenant within 2 months of the end
  date.
- **Source 2, clauses 6 and 7:** on a CPA lease the landlord must give notice 40–80
  business days before expiry, and expiry rolls to a monthly tenancy unless the
  tenant says otherwise.
- **Master: follows source 2**, and keeps source 1's renewal option as an additional
  right of the tenant, since it takes nothing away.

## 10. Notices

- **Source 1, clause 18:** *domicilium citandi et executandi*, hand delivery, fax,
  registered post (deemed 7 days), email.
- **Source 2, clause 27:** chosen addresses, hand, trackable delivery or email with
  evidence of receipt, "a bounced email is not effective delivery".
- **Master: merges both.** Source 2's delivery rules, with source 1's *domicilium*
  wording and the registered-post deeming provision retained.

## 11. Withholding rent

- Both documents forbid it (source 1 clause 23, source 2 clauses 10.5 and 24.3).
- **Master: follows source 2**, which is narrower — it preserves "any lawful right to
  remission, set off or relief from a tribunal or court" rather than excluding it.

## Blanks that are now real fields

Built in migration 0032, on the owner's instruction of 8 October 2026. Each has a
column to live in, an input on the form, and a place in the merge catalogue, so
it fills itself instead of being written in by hand:

| Field | Where it is entered |
|---|---|
| `term.deposit_refund_days` | Lease → Schedule details |
| `term.defects_notice_days` | Lease → Schedule details |
| `money.maintenance_callout_fee` | Lease → Schedule details |
| `money.early_cancellation_cap` | Lease → Schedule details |
| `rules.named_occupants` | Lease → Schedule details |
| `bank.payment_reference` | Lease → Schedule details |
| `refund.account_holder`, `refund.bank`, `refund.branch_code`, `refund.account_number` | Lease → Schedule details |
| `agent.registration_number`, `agent.practitioner`, `agent.certificate_number` | Settings → Landlord particulars |

The deposit refund account is the **tenant's** bank account, so the number is
sealed with the same authenticated encryption as the landlord's and only its last
four digits are ever shown again — on the agreement and on the form. Leaving the
field empty keeps the stored number rather than erasing it, because the form
never shows the whole thing back.

`rules.complaints_threshold` was deliberately NOT built: the master follows source
2 on complaints, no clause uses it, and a field nothing reads is a blank an
operator fills in for nothing.
